# Field notes from building agents. One observation per line.
# Lines starting with # are ignored. Append to the bottom, do not rewrite history.
Agents that retry silently are worse than agents that fail loudly. We lost two days to a retry loop swallowing a 401, and the fix was making every failure write a reason to the row it belongs to.
The bottleneck in agent work is almost never the model. It is the material going in. We spent a week tuning a prompt that was fine and one afternoon fixing the feed that was guessing.
Dedupe on content, not on identifier. Half of what we pulled twice had a new id and the same words, and the agent happily wrote the same post about it a second time.
Storing why a model picked something turned out to be worth more than storing what it wrote. A reviewer can judge a bad pick in two seconds and a bad sentence in twenty.
Human approval queues rot when the reviewer has to reconstruct context. Put the source material next to the draft or nobody reads either.
Every autonomous system we have run needed a way to skip rather than a way to improvise. Given a gap to fill, an agent will fill it, and what it fills it with is the problem.
A scheduler that publishes at a fixed time is easy. A scheduler that does nothing when there is nothing approved is the part people forget to build.
We stopped letting the generation layer post-process its own output. Every cleanup pass we added moved the failure somewhere harder to see.
Rate limits are a design constraint, not an error case. Treating a 429 as a bug produced worse behaviour than treating it as a scheduling input.
The last ten things published is the cheapest repetition guard we have found. Anything longer and the model starts writing around its own history instead of from the material.
Config that the content person can edit without a deploy changed how often the content actually got tuned. It went from monthly to twice a week.
Fingerprints beat timestamps for knowing what is new. Timestamps lie whenever a source rebuilds its feed.
An agent with one job and a narrow contract is easier to debug than an agent with a plan. We keep taking planning back out.
Validation that rewrites is validation that hides. Ours only reports, and the pipeline decides what to do about it.
